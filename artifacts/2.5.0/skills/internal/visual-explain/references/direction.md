# The line and the window

The owner approved Opus 5.5's direction on 2026-10-15 after rejecting two
earlier design rounds. No tables or tabs. Existing grill HTML remains intact.

One work line carries meaningful stages and genuine authorization boundaries.
A window focuses one stage; two connected annotations explain its scope or
evidence. Plans and deliveries use the same visual grammar with different
facts. A fixed drawing is not proof that a step happened.

At the 728px T3 reading width: a 24px title, 16px introduction, compact horizontal
line, movable outlined window with a faint host-accent tint, and two unboxed
annotations with 26px heavy headings. Status has shape and a text label, not
color alone. Focus does not hide stage labels. The only animation moves the
window in 200ms; reduced motion removes it. T3 supplies the font, colors and
theme. The exterior stays transparent with no banner, card or horizontal padding.

At narrow widths the line becomes vertical. Labels remain readable, and the
two annotations sit immediately below the selected stage. Stage buttons work
with click/tap and arrow keys, Home and End; the desktop window can be dragged.
Targets are at least 44px. Without JavaScript, show the initial stage and its
annotations as a static reading, with controls disabled.

References and their roles:

- [Maggie Appleton: Drawing Invisible Programming Concepts](https://maggieappleton.com/drawinginvisibles1)
  supplies the focused frame and annotations explaining what a representation
  highlights or leaves out. Do not copy the original illustration or lettering.
- [Bartosz Ciechanowski: Moon](https://ciechanow.ski/moon/) supplies a visual
  mechanism developed alongside concise explanatory prose.
- [Nicky Case: Explorable Explanations](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/)
  supplies purposeful interaction that reveals a relationship or process.

These references govern composition, not factual claims or user approval of
future work. Keep the chosen mechanism; adapt its stages and words to the task.
