## What the pilot actually says

The anonymous reviewer preferred Variant 02, which the withheld key maps to the
published-current skill condition (A). Variant 03, the article-direct condition
(B), was the most structurally distinct, but its large editorial introduction
and date competed with the clinical task. Variant 01 was corrected skills (C).

Therefore this one run does **not** show that explicitly mentioning the article
made Impeccable better. It does show that the article prompt can push structural
novelty. A plausible explanation for better results in other sessions is prompt
priming: naming the article injects a specific broad-explore / reference-led /
critic-led method at the moment of work, especially when the canonical skill was
not actually discovered or loaded. That explanation is not isolated by this
pilot because exact resolved model identity and run-to-run variance were not
controlled.

The durable correction is to encode the useful method in the skills and verify
their real loading. The article URL is not needed at runtime, and repeating it
should not be treated as a hidden activation command.
